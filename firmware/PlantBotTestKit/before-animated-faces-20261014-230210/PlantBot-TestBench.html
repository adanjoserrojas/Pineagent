1 · Board, PSRAM, LED

Connect, then run the checks. Needs only the USB cable.
LED pin
If something fails
  • No reply to PING: you're on the wrong USB port or the firmware isn't flashed. Use the port marked USB (native, shows as "USB JTAG/serial debug unit" or "USB Serial Device"). The COM/UART port only carries boot logs.
  • PSRAM 0 MB: in Arduino IDE set PSRAM → OPI PSRAM and re-flash. If it still says 0, that board is bad; swap in a spare from the 5-pack.
  • Flash reads 16 MB: it's an N16R8. Fine for us; set Flash Size → 16MB next time you flash.
  • LED stays dark: click pin 38 and try a colour again. Some clones wire the RGB LED there.

2 · 5 V brick sense

– pin – mV unknown
The ADC tops out near 3.1 V, so a healthy 5 V rail shows as about ≥ 4.6 V. That's expected: this pin only answers "is the brick on?"
If something fails
  • Reads 0 with the brick on: check the brick's switch/plug, the 10k from +5V to GPIO7, and the 20k from GPIO7 to GND.
  • Reads high with the brick off: the 20k to GND is missing, so the pin is floating. Or the brick's +5V is touching the board's 5V pin (it must not).
  • Never wire the brick straight to GPIO7 without the divider: 5 V on a GPIO kills it.

3 · Face display

Fill invert
Backlight 255
If something fails
  • Completely dark: backlight. Check BLK→GPIO21 and VCC→3V3. Drag the backlight slider; if it never lights, the BLK wire or VCC is off.
  • Lit but blank/white: data lines. Check SCL→12, SDA→11, CS→10, DC→9, RES→14. SCL/SDA swapped is the most common mistake.
  • Black shows white, red shows cyan: click invert off. Some clone panels ship un-inverted.
  • Red and blue swapped: the panel is BGR; tell the firmware dev (one register change).
  • Red frame missing on one edge / garbage strip: wrong offset; the panel may not be 170×320. Measure it and tell us.
  • Speed test should report well above 15 fps. Much lower means a bad joint on SCL.

4 · Arms (servos)

Needs the 5 V brick plugged in. Arms are limited to 20–160° so they never grind into their end stops.
Left arm (GPIO15) 90°
Right arm (GPIO16) 90°
If something fails
  • "needs the 5 V brick": plug the brick in; card 2 must say brick on.
  • Doesn't move, no buzz: servo red/brown wires aren't getting brick 5 V/GND. Check the power board.
  • Twitches randomly or jitters: missing common ground between the brick GND and the ESP32 GND. The signal needs a shared reference.
  • Board disconnects during Wave: brown-out. The servos are pulling the rail down. Check the 1000 µF capacitor polarity/placement and that servos are NOT on the ESP32's 5V pin.
  • Moves the "wrong" way: that's fine; it's a mounting thing and is fixed in software.

5 · Speaker (I2S amp)

Needs the 5 V brick. Start quiet: volume is % of full scale.
Hz ms vol
If something fails
  • Silent: amp VIN must come from the brick's +5V and its GND to common ground. Then check BCLK→4, LRC→5, DIN→6 (BCLK/LRC swapped = silence).
  • Silent, wiring correct: the amp's SD pin must not be tied to GND (leave it unconnected). Speaker wires go to the amp's +/− terminals, never to GND.
  • Distorted at higher volume: lower the vol. Without a GAIN connection the amp is at 9 dB, and small speakers distort easily.
  • Constant hum/whine: missing 100 µF + 0.1 µF at the amp VIN, or the ground is daisy-chained instead of star-wired.
  • Board disconnects on loud tones: brown-out; same fix as the servos.

6 · Piezo chirp

If something fails
  • Silent: check GPIO17 → piezo + and the piezo − → GND.
  • Same pitch for both beeps: it's an active buzzer (has a built-in oscillator). It works, but it can't chirp. A passive piezo is the right part.
  • Very quiet: normal for a bare piezo disc. 2.7–4 kHz is its loudest range.

How to use

  1. Flash PlantBotTest.ino (see README).
  2. Plug the USB (native) port into the laptop, click Connect, pick the port.
  3. Go top to bottom: board and display first on USB only, then plug in the brick for power, arms and speaker.
  4. Mark each check Pass/Fail. Results are kept per robot in this browser.
